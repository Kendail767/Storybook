The cemetery sits on a small hill overlooking the village. Brightly painted tombs and crosses glow in the sun. Fresh flowers on some graves. Others, older, covered in moss.

An old woman kneels by a grave. She doesn't look up as you approach.

"They say the dead know things," she whispers. "My husband knew Tata Duente when they were boys. Said he was a forest spirit, not evil, just... fair. You respect the bush, he respect you. You disrespect, he curse you."

She finally looks at you. Her eyes are milky with age.

"Someone cut down a ceiba tree three months ago. Young fool from the city, thought he'd sell the wood. Found dead two weeks later. No mark on him. Just... stopped living."

She turns back to the grave.

"Tata Duente doesn't forget."
